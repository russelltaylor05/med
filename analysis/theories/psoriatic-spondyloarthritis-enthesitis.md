# Theory: Psoriatic Spondyloarthritis With Enthesitis

Last updated: 2026-08-08

## Status

This remains the strongest chronic inflammatory unifier, but it is no longer the leading overall explanation and is not established. The current center of gravity is a two-hit model of mechanical tendon load intolerance plus isotretinoin-associated musculoskeletal effects. Psoriatic/axial disease stays active because blood tests alone cannot exclude it and the psoriasis/nail history is genuine risk context.

The missing piece is objective evidence: a convincing inflammatory history and examination, Doppler-positive/erosive enthesitis, synovitis/dactylitis, reduced axial/chest mobility, or characteristic SI imaging.

## Why It Fits

- Psoriasis diagnosed for more than 10 years and nail pitting/ridges for roughly 10–15 years.
- Symptom onset before age 45 and duration over three months.
- Pain near possible attachment sites: proximal hamstring origins/sit bones, medial elbow and rib/chest wall, with hip/glute/lower-back and neck/back stiffness.
- Morning stiffness of about 15–60 minutes and worsening after sitting/inactivity.
- Psoriatic arthritis can be RF/CCP negative, HLA-B27 negative and have normal ESR/CRP.
- PsA can involve entheses and axial/SI structures without the symmetric small-joint pattern typical of rheumatoid arthritis.

## Why It Is Below The Leading Model

- By 2026-06-16 a rheumatologist reportedly did not think this was autoimmune, and on 2026-08-08 the patient reported multiple clinicians felt the blood did not indicate autoimmune disease. Their notes and exact examinations are unavailable.
- No recorded clinician has documented synovitis, dactylitis, active inflammatory enthesitis, sacroiliitis, reduced chest expansion or restricted spinal mobility.
- No Achilles/plantar-fascia pattern, swollen digits, uveitis or inflammatory-bowel symptoms are recorded.
- ESR 2, hsCRP 0.29, earlier CRP <3, normal CBC and albumin provide repeated evidence against a high systemic inflammatory burden.
- The most focal lower-body symptoms are strongly provoked by sitting, hamstring stretching/yoga and squatting—classic proximal-hamstring compression/loading mechanisms.
- Medial elbow pain began after rowing before the latest isotretinoin course.
- The broader syndrome began during isotretinoin exposure, which can mimic inflammatory back pain, tendinitis and even MRI-positive sacroiliitis.
- The thoracic x-ray showed mild degenerative change only. It does not assess SI joints or early inflammatory disease, but it offers no positive inflammatory support.

## How To Interpret The “Negative” Blood

- **RF/anti-CCP/anti-MCV:** meaningfully lower seropositive RA, not PsA.
- **HLA-B27:** a negative result lowers classic HLA-B27-associated axial SpA probability, but many patients with axial PsA are negative.
- **ESR/CRP:** repeated normal values lower the probability and likely burden of active systemic disease, but normal CRP is common in clinically active PsA and guidelines advise against using normal markers as an exclusion test.
- **ANA AC-2:** this is not a PsA test. An isolated confirmed anti-DFS70 profile would lower concern for lupus/Sjogren/systemic sclerosis, not adjudicate psoriatic arthritis.

Therefore, “blood does not indicate autoimmune disease” is accurate for several blood-supported systemic diseases and for high systemic inflammation. It is too strong if intended to mean that blood alone excludes localized psoriatic enthesitis/axial disease. Several normal expert examinations plus negative targeted imaging would be much more persuasive.

## History And Examination Features That Would Move It Up

- Second-half-of-night back/buttock pain that improves after getting up.
- Alternating buttock/SI pain and clear improvement with exercise but not rest.
- Marked response to a clinician-supervised full-dose NSAID trial within roughly 48 hours, recognizing this is not specific.
- Reduced chest expansion or spinal mobility, SI findings, objective enthesis swelling/tenderness in a coherent pattern.
- Dactylitis, swollen joints, Achilles/plantar-fascia inflammation, current active psoriasis/nail dystrophy, uveitis, IBD or first-degree family history.

## Imaging Strategy

- Obtain the rheumatology note first and determine whether the relevant inflammatory examination was actually normal.
- If several inflammatory features remain genuine and the result would change management, discuss a one-time dedicated unenhanced SI-joint MRI using an inflammatory-back-pain protocol. NICE describes SI radiographs followed by MRI when radiographs are negative/nondiagnostic; clinicians may go directly to MRI in selected early-disease settings.
- The existing thoracic x-ray cannot evaluate SI inflammation, proximal hamstring disease or deep-gluteal structures.
- SI MRI is not binary. Small isolated bone-marrow-edema foci occur after mechanical strain and in healthy/athletic people. Distribution, depth, number of slices, structural lesions, history and examination all matter; isotretinoin exposure and the June squat flare should be disclosed to radiology.
- Power-Doppler ultrasound can help at accessible focal entheses such as the medial elbow. Tendon thickening/hypoechogenicity alone is not specific and can be mechanical. Doppler signal and erosions are more specific but not sensitive; a quiet ultrasound cannot fully exclude PsA.
- The proximal hamstring origin is deep and may be better assessed by dedicated pelvic/hamstring MRI when the main question is tendinopathy/partial tear rather than systemic enthesitis.

## Limited Laboratory Follow-Up

Repeating ANA, RF/CCP or broad inflammatory panels is unlikely to resolve this theory. If the ANA has never been clinically closed and connective-tissue symptoms exist—photosensitive rash, ulcers, Raynaud phenomenon, dry eyes/mouth, inflammatory swollen joints, cytopenias, kidney/urine abnormalities—one disease-directed evaluation could include confirmation of anti-DFS70 plus ENA/dsDNA, complements and urinalysis. Without those clinical features, serial broad testing risks false leads.

## Treatment Paths If Objectively Confirmed

- Exercise/physical therapy, smoking avoidance if applicable, and symptom-directed NSAIDs when medically safe remain foundational.
- Mechanical tendon rehabilitation may still be required even if PsA is present.
- Rheumatology treatment depends on whether disease is axial, peripheral, enthesitis-heavy and/or skin/nail dominant. Biologic or targeted therapy should follow a confident diagnosis and objective activity, not a positive ANA or nonspecific pain alone.
- For presumed isotretinoin-associated transient sacroiliitis without chronic inflammatory features, stopping the drug and conservative treatment are generally discussed before immunosuppression.

## What Would Strengthen This Theory

- Reproducible inflammatory history plus clinician-confirmed enthesitis/synovitis/dactylitis or reduced axial/chest mobility.
- SI MRI with a convincing inflammatory pattern or chronic structural changes.
- Doppler-positive/erosive enthesitis in a distribution fitting PsA.
- Development of uveitis, IBD, heel enthesitis or other SpA features.
- Persistence/progression well beyond isotretinoin withdrawal despite successful correction of mechanical load.

## What Would Weaken This Theory

- Actual notes confirm several normal specialist examinations covering entheses, SI joints, spine/chest, nails/skin and peripheral joints.
- Dedicated SI MRI is negative or only shows small nonspecific strain-related lesions, and targeted Doppler ultrasound is quiet.
- A sports examination reproduces a coherent mechanical hamstring/elbow pattern that improves steadily with graded rehabilitation.
- Symptoms resolve after isotretinoin withdrawal and load correction without immunomodulatory treatment.
