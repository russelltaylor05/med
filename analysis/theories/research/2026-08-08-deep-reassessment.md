## Isotretinoin Evidence

### Label evidence

The current U.S. label includes back/neck/extremity pain, arthralgia, myalgia, stiffness, tendonitis, arthritis, transient chest pain, CK elevation/rare rhabdomyolysis, calcification and hyperostosis. It advises assessment of symptoms occurring during or after a course and notes approximately 16% musculoskeletal symptoms in one trial. Label inclusion establishes a recognized adverse-event signal, not patient-specific causation.

- DailyMed/FDA label, revised July 2026: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31f62a49-4c6d-4ce3-a0ca-d159562370b1&version=2

### Sacroiliitis evidence

- A prospective controlled study of 100 isotretinoin-treated and 101 antibiotic-treated acne patients found inflammatory back pain in 21 and MRI sacroiliitis in 11 isotretinoin patients, versus none in controls; most regressed after cessation. Strength: prospective comparison. Limits: single center, young cohort, high event rate requiring replication; not a general incidence estimate. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35092165/
- A 2025 aggregation of 67 published cases found median onset 2.5 months, frequent HLA-B27 negativity and often normal ESR/CRP; most recovered, with a reported range extending to 48 weeks. Strength: shows phenotype and that quiet blood is possible. Limits: selected case reports, publication bias, no denominator. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12108023/
- A 2026 retrospective MRI comparison reported less extensive edema and fewer chronic structural lesions in isotretinoin-associated cases than axial SpA. Strength: directly addresses interpretation. Limits: retrospective and group differences. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11845-026-04414-y
- A small 2025 historical cohort found that some initially attributed cases later met axial-SpA criteria, including a patient with psoriasis. This supports continued follow-up when objective disease persists, not a claim that isotretinoin causes PsA. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40455047/

### Tendon and muscle evidence

- A cross-sectional controlled study found frequent back pain and 4.3% Achilles tendinopathy among isotretinoin patients, with no tendinopathy in controls. Small samples and high symptom rates limit generalization. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32977793/
- A controlled ultrasound study of 30 treated and 30 control patients found thicker quadriceps/patellar/Achilles entheses and higher GUESS scores after treatment. It was cross-sectional without individual pretreatment baseline; it supports biological plausibility but not proximal-hamstring causation. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39680093/
- A prospective study found no meaningful loss of measured quadriceps/hamstring strength over a six-month course. This weakens persistent generalized muscle weakness as the main mechanism. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7981891/

### Hyperostosis evidence

Retinoid hyperostosis is real but is most strongly associated with prolonged/high exposure. Multiple ordinary acne courses may increase interest, but clinically important symptomatic hyperostosis cannot be inferred from three courses. The thoracic report did not describe it.

- Hyperostosis review/case: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4447744/
- Long-term low-dose randomized study: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1626958/
- Long-term radiographic follow-up: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2968377/

## Proximal Hamstring / Mechanical Evidence

Proximal hamstring tendinopathy commonly causes lower-buttock/ischial pain with sitting, running/lunging, hip flexion, hamstring contraction and stretching. The recorded yoga/stretch, sitting and squat triggers are a strong clinical match. It can be bilateral, and normal blood work is expected.

- A 2025 randomized trial of 100 participants found both individualized physiotherapy and shockwave groups improved, with no significant difference in primary outcomes through 52 weeks. It supports structured rehabilitation and does not establish shockwave superiority. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41243328/
- A systematic review found the intervention evidence base low/very low quality, limiting confident claims about injections or surgery. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8016446/
- MRI studies show some proximal-hamstring signal abnormalities in asymptomatic people; tendon enlargement and peritendinous edema correlate better with symptoms. Imaging must match the examination. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22268187/
- ACR chronic-hip imaging criteria consider expert ultrasound or noncontrast MRI appropriate for suspected extra-articular tendon/bursal disease after nondiagnostic radiographs. https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69425/Narrative/
- Deep-gluteal review for adjacent sciatic entrapment differential: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4718497/

## PsA/Enthesis Imaging Evidence And Caveats

- A dedicated SI-joint inflammatory MRI is reasonable when a genuine inflammatory history/examination remains and the result would change management. Another thoracic x-ray will not answer this question.
- Small SI marrow-edema lesions can be caused by strain and occur in athletes/healthy people. MRI should be read by an MSK radiologist and interpreted with loading history, isotretinoin exposure and structural lesions. https://rmdopen.bmj.com/content/4/1/e000586
- Ultrasound tendon thickening or hypoechogenicity is not specific for inflammatory enthesitis. A 2024 meta-analysis found only moderate grayscale diagnostic performance; power Doppler was highly specific but insensitive. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39705203/
- The DEUS multicenter study found power Doppler and erosions among the most discriminating lesions. A quiet scan does not exclude PsA. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38443140/

## EBV Evidence

VCA IgM+/VCA IgG+/EBNA IgG+ is not the classic early primary pattern. CDC describes primary infection as VCA IgM without EBNA; all-three-positive profiles can reflect late primary/convalescence, persistent IgM, reactivation or nonspecific IgM. High IgG titers can persist and do not measure activity.

- CDC interpretation: https://www.cdc.gov/epstein-barr/php/laboratories/index.html
- Mayo profile: https://www.mayocliniclabs.com/test-catalog/overview/621373
- Triple-positive study: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17336144/
- Serology problem-solving review: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3782265/

Absent classic mono symptoms, cytopenia/lymphocytosis or liver abnormalities, serial EBV titers/PCR are low yield in an immunocompetent patient and EBV is not a credible six-month unifier.

## Rare/Conditional Mimics

- **SAPHO/chronic nonbacterial osteitis:** ask about sternoclavicular/manubriosternal swelling, palm/sole pustules and exact severe-acne subtype before targeted imaging. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8802951/
- **Hypermobility spectrum:** only elevate with lifelong flexibility, recurrent sprains/subluxations, instability, soft-tissue injury or family history. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10457547/
- **Nociplastic/fibromyalgia overlay:** only elevate with generalized hypersensitivity plus unrefreshing sleep, fatigue and cognitive/sensory symptoms. It can amplify genuine tendon pain and should not be used dismissively. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33974577/
- **Fluoroquinolone/statin/steroid effects:** obtain a full 12-month medication history. No exposure is currently documented.
- **Celiac disease:** tTG-IgA plus total IgA while eating gluten only if GI symptoms, dermatitis herpetiformis, deficiency, fracture/osteopenia or family history. https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/professionals/clinical-tools-patient-management/digestive-diseases/celiac-disease-health-care-professionals
- **B12/phosphate/PTH:** test only with neurologic/dietary risks, true proximal weakness, bone pain, stress fractures or other specific clues. Current broad metabolic workup is reassuring.

## Separate Vertigo/Ear-Pressure Track

Ear popping followed by motion-sickness-type dizziness and the December weeks-long positional episode may be separate from the pain syndrome. Eustachian-tube dysfunction/barotrauma is plausible at 6,000 feet; BPPV, vestibular neuritis and inner-ear disease depend on timing, triggers and hearing symptoms. If it recurs, otoscopy, tympanometry, audiogram and a positional/neurologic examination are more useful than autoimmune blood tests.

Isotretinoin labeling includes dizziness, but a small prospective vestibular study did not find a clear objective vestibulo-ocular reflex effect, so this is not a strong unifier. Severe headache/nausea with visual symptoms after isotretinoin, especially with tetracycline exposure, requires urgent assessment for intracranial hypertension.
