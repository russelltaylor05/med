# Theory: Psoriatic Spondyloarthritis With Enthesitis

Last updated: 2026-06-08

## Status

This is the strongest current working theory, not a diagnosis.

The working idea is: psoriatic arthritis or psoriatic spondyloarthritis with enthesitis, possibly with axial involvement around the sacroiliac joints/spine/rib cage. Isotretinoin-related musculoskeletal symptoms and mechanical overuse may be contributing or confounding factors.

The finalized 2026-06-03 EBV panel adds a possible recent infection/recovery/reactivation layer. That may contribute to general malaise or symptom amplification if the timing fits, but it does not replace the psoriatic/enthesitis question because the dominant symptoms started months earlier and are concentrated at tendon/attachment and axial/chest-wall regions.

## Why This Fits

- Longstanding psoriasis, not recent, for over 10 years.
- Nail pitting/ridges for around 15 years, roughly since 2011.
- Pain pattern includes tendon/attachment-type areas: hamstring attachments/sit-bone region, medial elbow, hips/glutes/lower back, and rib/chest wall.
- Morning stiffness lasts about 15-60 minutes.
- Symptoms are worse after sitting/inactivity.
- Heat helps.
- Age is under 45 during symptom development.
- RF, CCP, and MCV antibodies are negative, which lowers support for classic seropositive rheumatoid arthritis.
- HLA-B27 is negative and CRP is normal, but those do not rule out axial spondyloarthritis or psoriatic arthritis.
- Rib/back-related evening chest pain episodes on 2026-05-26 and 2026-05-27 may fit chest wall/rib involvement, though chest pain must not be assumed to be inflammatory without medical evaluation.

## Why This Is Not Certain

- No clinician exam has documented enthesitis, synovitis, sacroiliitis, dactylitis, restricted chest expansion, or reduced spinal mobility yet.
- No SI joint/pelvis imaging or MRI is available yet. Thoracic spine x-ray on 2026-06-08 showed mild degenerative changes only, without fracture, dislocation, lytic/blastic lesion, or high-grade disc height loss.
- No clear Achilles, plantar fascia, knee, or sausage-digit symptoms have been reported.
- No major fatigue, inflammatory bowel symptoms, or eye inflammation symptoms have been reported.
- Normal CRP and negative HLA-B27 reduce some supporting evidence.
- Isotretinoin can cause back pain, joint pain, muscle pain, stiffness, tendonitis, arthritis-like symptoms, and transient chest pain, so it could mimic or amplify this pattern.
- Recent EBV infection or recovery/reactivation could be a confounder if there was a mono-like illness or abrupt symptom worsening near the 2026-06-03 blood draw.
- The ANA is positive at 1:320, but the dense fine speckled pattern is less specific for lupus/Sjogren's/systemic sclerosis than many other ANA patterns and may be incidental, especially if isolated.

## Research Notes

- NIAMS describes psoriatic arthritis as inflammatory disease of joints and entheses. It notes that most people who develop psoriatic arthritis already have psoriasis, and that symptoms can include spine-related stiffness, enthesis tenderness, nail pitting/crumbling, dactylitis, uveitis, and inflammatory bowel disease.
- NIAMS says there is no definitive test for psoriatic arthritis. Diagnosis is based on symptoms, physical exam, skin/nail findings, blood tests to rule out other disease, and imaging such as X-ray, ultrasound, or MRI to evaluate joints, entheses, or spine.
- The American College of Rheumatology patient sheet says psoriatic arthritis can involve the back or pelvis, nail pitting, tendon inflammation called enthesitis, colitis, and uveitis. It also lists treatment categories including NSAIDs, corticosteroid injections, DMARDs, biologics, and targeted oral medications.
- The Spondylitis Association of America notes that non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis has no single clear diagnostic test. HLA-B27 and inflammatory markers can help, but not everyone has HLA-B27 positivity or elevated inflammatory markers. MRI of SI joints can show inflammation not visible on X-ray.
- The Spondylitis Association of America describes inflammatory back pain features: onset under 45, symptoms lasting over 3 months, worse with immobility/night/early morning, and easing with activity/exercise. It also highlights exam of SI joints, chest, heels, spinal mobility, and chest expansion.
- ASAS-EULAR 2022 axial spondyloarthritis recommendations discuss escalation to biologic or targeted synthetic DMARDs, such as TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, and JAK inhibitors, after inadequate response to NSAIDs and when objective inflammation is present by CRP, MRI, or radiographic sacroiliitis.
- DailyMed isotretinoin labeling reports musculoskeletal symptoms during or after therapy, including back pain, arthralgia, musculoskeletal pain/stiffness, tendonitis, arthritis, and transient chest pain. It also notes bilirubin and liver enzyme abnormalities can occur, relevant because total bilirubin was mildly high on 2026-05-07.
- The finalized 2026-06-03 Quest report shows EBV VCA IgM, VCA IgG, and EBNA IgG all positive, with Quest interpreting the pattern as suggestive of recent EBV infection. CDC and Mayo interpretation notes emphasize that EBV antibody patterns need clinical context; all-three-positive patterns can suggest recent recovery or reactivation rather than a simple early primary infection.
- DFS70/dense fine speckled ANA literature generally treats isolated anti-DFS70 as less suggestive of ANA-associated systemic autoimmune rheumatic disease, but it does not absolutely rule out connective tissue disease. Follow-up ENA/anti-dsDNA/complements/urinalysis should be driven by clinical context.

## What Would Strengthen This Theory

- Rheumatology exam showing enthesitis, inflammatory back pain pattern, limited spine mobility, reduced chest expansion, SI joint tenderness, synovitis, dactylitis, or active nail/skin psoriasis.
- MRI pelvis/SI joints showing active sacroiliitis or inflammatory changes.
- Ultrasound or MRI showing enthesitis at symptomatic sites.
- Clear improvement with a clinician-supervised NSAID trial.
- Development or recognition of related features: heel pain, plantar fascia pain, alternating buttock pain, uveitis, inflammatory bowel symptoms, swollen joints, dactylitis, or strong family history.

## What Would Weaken This Theory

- Normal rheumatology exam with no enthesitis, synovitis, SI tenderness, chest expansion issue, or spinal mobility restriction.
- MRI SI joints/spine without inflammatory findings, especially if symptoms remain mild and non-progressive.
- Symptoms steadily resolve after stopping isotretinoin and reducing provocative mechanical loading.
- Symptoms clearly track with a recent mono-like illness and resolve as the EBV illness resolves, without ongoing enthesitis, inflammatory back pain, or imaging/exam findings.
- Clear mechanical diagnosis from sports medicine/physical therapy that explains symptoms without inflammatory features.

## Workup To Discuss

- Rheumatology referral focused on psoriatic arthritis, axial spondyloarthritis, and enthesitis.
- Exam: SI joints, hips, spine mobility, chest expansion, ribs/costochondral joints, heels/plantar fascia, elbows, hamstring origins, nails, scalp/groin psoriasis, and peripheral joints.
- Imaging: pelvis/SI joint X-ray or MRI, with MRI favored if early/non-radiographic axial disease is suspected.
- Consider ultrasound or MRI of focal enthesis sites if the physical exam is unclear.
- Labs: ESR, repeat CRP, CBC, CMP, urinalysis, urine protein/creatinine, ENA panel, anti-dsDNA, C3/C4, and possibly anti-DFS70.
- Bilirubin follow-up: repeat total bilirubin with direct and indirect fractions.
- EBV context: ask the ordering clinician whether CBC with differential, liver enzymes, spleen exam/activity precautions, repeat EBV serology, EBV early antigen, or EBV DNA PCR are warranted based on symptoms.

## Treatment Paths To Discuss

- If safe for the patient, a clinician-supervised NSAID trial can help symptoms and may provide diagnostic signal.
- Physical therapy should avoid aggressive stretching as the main strategy if it worsens symptoms. Prefer graded tendon loading, hip/core strengthening, gentle mobility, breathing/chest wall mobility, and rowing/activity modifications.
- If confirmed and persistent: rheumatology may consider DMARDs or biologics depending on whether the dominant pattern is axial, peripheral, enthesitis-heavy, skin/nail-heavy, or mixed.
- For prominent axial disease: NSAIDs, exercise/PT, TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, and in selected cases JAK inhibitors are commonly discussed categories.
- For prominent peripheral PsA/enthesitis/skin disease: options can include NSAIDs, local steroid injections, methotrexate/sulfasalazine/leflunomide, apremilast, TNF inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, IL-23/IL-12/23 pathway biologics, JAK inhibitors, and other rheumatologist-selected therapies.
- Discuss isotretinoin timing with dermatology/rheumatology. If symptoms are improving after stopping, that points toward medication contribution. If symptoms persist or progress, inflammatory disease remains higher on the list.
- If recent EBV/mono is clinically suspected, treatment is usually supportive and activity precautions may matter; coordinate with the ordering clinician before intense exercise or heavy lifting if spleen enlargement is a concern.

## Chest Pain Safety Note

The 2026-05-26 and 2026-05-27 chest pain episodes may have been chest wall/rib/back related, but chest pain can also come from heart, lung, reflux, anxiety, infection, or other causes. If chest pain recurs, especially with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, dizziness, palpitations, exertional worsening, fainting, or pain radiating to arm/jaw, seek urgent or emergency medical evaluation.
