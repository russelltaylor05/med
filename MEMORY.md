## Source-First Reassessment, 2026-06-16

This reassessment intentionally starts from `SYMPTOMS.md`, `HISTORY.md`, and the source test results rather than prior theory files. It incorporates the user report that, by 2026-06-16, rheumatology said they do not think this is an autoimmune disease.

### Overall read

- The current center of gravity is no longer "probable autoimmune disease." The most source-consistent read is a mixed musculoskeletal picture: mechanical/tendon loading plus possible isotretinoin-related musculoskeletal sensitivity, with a recent EBV-positive illness or recovery state possibly adding a temporary systemic layer.
- Autoimmune/inflammatory disease is not impossible, especially given psoriasis/nail history and morning stiffness, but the rheumatologist's impression plus quiet inflammatory labs lowers its priority unless new objective signs appear.
- The pain pattern deserves treatment and monitoring even if it is not autoimmune. "Not autoimmune" does not mean "nothing is happening"; it redirects the next workup toward mechanical diagnosis, medication timing, post-viral recovery, and functional rehab.

### Why autoimmune is lower priority now

- Rheumatology exam/assessment reportedly did not point toward autoimmune disease. That is important because exam findings such as synovitis, dactylitis, enthesitis, SI tenderness, reduced spinal mobility, rash/nail activity, or eye/GI features often carry more weight than screening labs alone.
- CRP was <3.0 mg/L, CK was 115 U/L, CBC with differential was within the report's reference ranges, RF/CCP/MCV antibodies were negative, HLA-B27 was negative, thyroid tests were in range, and hepatitis A/B/C testing was non-reactive.
- The ANA was positive at 1:320, but the pattern was nuclear dense fine speckled/AC-2. The Quest report notes this pattern is seen in normal individuals and is rarely associated with SLE, Sjogren's syndrome, and systemic sclerosis. Current literature and lab guidance similarly treat isolated DFS70/DFS-pattern ANA as less supportive of systemic autoimmune rheumatic disease when disease-specific antibodies and clinical features are absent.
- Psoriasis and nail pitting still matter, but without recorded swollen joints, sausage digits, uveitis, inflammatory bowel symptoms, Achilles/plantar heel pain, elevated inflammatory markers, or a rheumatologist seeing objective inflammatory findings, they are risk context rather than proof.

### Better-fitting active buckets

- Mechanical/proximal hamstring tendinopathy or related hip-pelvis tendon overload is now a leading bucket. It fits sit-bone/hamstring attachment pain, worsening with sitting/inactivity, worsening after yoga/stretching, and a disproportionate flare after light squats on 2026-06-09. Medial elbow pain after rowing also fits a separate overuse/tendinopathy mechanism.
- Isotretinoin-associated musculoskeletal symptoms remain a strong timing match. Back pain began around December 2025 while on a roughly November/December 2025 to May 1, 2026 Accutane course. Published and labeling sources include back pain, arthralgia, myalgia, tendinopathy, and rare sacroiliitis-like presentations among reported musculoskeletal adverse effects. Symptoms persisting after stopping does not prove or disprove this; trend since May 1 is a key clue.
- EBV/recent viral illness may explain the early-June low-energy/headache spell and some global flare sensitivity. The 2026-06-03 EBV panel had VCA IgM positive, VCA IgG positive, and EBNA IgG positive. Mayo Clinic Laboratories interprets this triple-positive pattern as possibly recent recovery or reactivation; CDC notes primary/new infection is more typical when VCA IgM is present without EBNA. The normal 2026-06-08 CBC and lack of recorded classic mono symptoms make EBV a weak explanation for the full 6-month tendon/back course, but it could contribute to feeling worse in June.
- Mild thoracic/lower cervical degenerative x-ray findings could contribute to local spine discomfort, but the report did not show fracture, dislocation, destructive lesion, abnormal alignment, or high-grade disc height loss. Mild degenerative findings do not explain the whole hamstring/glute/hip/elbow pattern by themselves.
- The December 2025 vertigo/nausea episode may be a separate vestibular event unless it recurs or connects to neurologic symptoms. It does not currently unify the musculoskeletal timeline.

### What would change the interpretation

- Autoimmune/inflammatory disease would move back up if there is objective joint swelling, dactylitis, clear inflammatory heel pain, uveitis, inflammatory bowel symptoms, persistent night/early-morning back pain improving after getting up, reduced chest expansion/spinal mobility, elevated ESR/CRP on repeat testing, or MRI/ultrasound evidence of active inflammation.
- Mechanical/tendinopathy would move up further if pain is reproducible with tendon loading, hip flexion/compression, sitting, squats/hinges/lunges, rowing mechanics, or specific lumbar/SI/hip provocation tests, and improves with a targeted graded loading plan.
- Isotretinoin effect would move up if symptoms clearly began or escalated after starting/increasing dose and gradually improve over weeks to months after stopping; it would move down if symptoms keep progressively worsening far beyond discontinuation without another explanation.
- EBV would move up if there were clear mono-like features, abnormal liver enzymes, splenic enlargement/tenderness, atypical lymphocytes, or a clinician felt the serology fits a symptomatic recent infection/recovery state.

### Focused Note: Mechanical Spine Contribution, 2026-06-12

- Mechanical spine issues remain a plausible contributor, especially for local mid-back/lower-back discomfort and pain that worsens with specific positions, bending, twisting, lifting, rowing mechanics, prolonged sitting posture, or deconditioning.
- The 2026-06-08 thoracic x-ray showing mild lower cervical/thoracic degenerative changes provides a possible mechanical pain generator, but mild degenerative findings are common and may be incidental. The report did not show fracture, dislocation, lytic/blastic lesion, abnormal alignment, or high-grade disc height loss.
- Mechanical spine findings alone are a weaker fit for the whole pattern because the symptoms include hamstring/sit-bone attachment-region pain, medial elbow tendinitis, hips/glutes/lower-back stiffness, rib/chest-wall tightness, morning stiffness, worse symptoms after rest/inactivity, longstanding psoriasis, and long-term nail pitting/ridges.
- The most reasonable current estimate is mixed: mechanical spine/tendon loading may explain or amplify part of the pain, but it is less likely to be the single unifying explanation unless exam/PT assessment finds a clear mechanical pattern and symptoms improve with targeted mechanical treatment.
- If the "worse after rest/inactivity" feature is removed or treated as uncertain, confidence in inflammatory back pain/axial spondyloarthritis decreases meaningfully. Mechanical spine/tendon loading becomes more competitive as an explanation, but psoriasis/nail history, multi-site attachment-region pain, rib/chest-wall symptoms, and morning stiffness still keep inflammatory enthesitis/psoriatic arthritis on the differential.
- A clinician or physical therapist could help distinguish this by checking neurologic signs, spine/SI/hip range of motion, facet/disc provocation, hamstring origin/adductor/gluteal tendons, rowing mechanics, and response to a graded strengthening/mobility plan.
- Findings that would increase confidence in a mechanical explanation: reproducible pain with specific movements or loading, clear focal tenderness in muscles/facets/discs/tendons, improvement with rest or position changes, no inflammatory exam findings, normal SI/pelvis imaging if performed, and steady improvement with targeted PT/activity modification.
- Findings that would decrease confidence in a purely mechanical explanation: night pain or early-morning pain that improves after getting up, prolonged morning stiffness, alternating buttock pain, reduced chest expansion, objective enthesitis/synovitis/dactylitis, uveitis/inflammatory bowel symptoms, or MRI evidence of sacroiliitis/active inflammation.
